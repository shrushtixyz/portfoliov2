Increasing Sell-Through Rate for Manual Sales at Seatgeek

TLDR;

Problem:
Sellers had data, but still couldn’t decide what to price. Tickets sat too high, recommendations didn’t feel trustworthy, and Smart Pricing wasn’t sticking.
Target audience:
Manual sellers on SeatGeek, desktop and mobile.
Goal:
Make the recommendation the thing sellers actually understand and act on.
Impact:
$980K incremental annual revenue. Selling rate +8.37%. Adoption +4.38%.

What I did

  • Shipped 2 pricing experiments across desktop and mobile.
  • Ran 5 JTBD interviews and paired them with the numbers we already had.
  • Defined states, edge cases, and fallbacks with Product, Eng, Research, Design, and Legal.
  • Prototyped end-to-end concepts before asking engineering to build.

Overview

Pricing is hard. The product made it harder.

Selling tickets means guessing price, timing, and demand all at once. SeatGeek already had recommendations — sellers just weren’t using them the way we hoped.

Problem

More information wasn’t helping

After auditing the flow and talking to PMs, designers, engineers, and sellers, three things kept showing up:

  • Sellers priced too high.
  • They didn’t trust why we recommended a number.
  • Smart Pricing adoption stayed inconsistent.

Defining

First idea: give them more context

Hypothesis: sellers price better if we give them data they can trust.

Iterating

The plan changed — and that was the point

Recent Sales piled on complexity. Worse: recent sale prices were above our recommendation about 60–66% of the time. More comps weren’t going to fix trust.

With product and engineering, we agreed on a tighter plan: influence the pricing decision first. Smaller surface. Clearer bet.

Instead of:

"How can we give sellers more data?"

I asked:

"How do we change what sellers actually do at the moment of pricing?"

Solution

Make the recommendation impossible to miss

Sellers needed one clear read:

Where the market is → Where they are → Where they should be

So I stopped adding insights and made the recommendation the product.

Decisions I called:

  1. Recommendation is the headline, not a footnote
  2. Show just enough reasoning — not a data dump
  3. Use the bar to ground them, without taking away their choice

I defined the states, missing-data cases, and fallbacks so it could ship. It’s live and collecting data.

$980K

Estimated annualized revenue impact after shipping mobile and desktop. Sell-through +8.37%. Adoption +3.87% in a month.
