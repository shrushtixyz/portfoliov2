Driving more sales for Manual Pricing at Seatgeek

TLDR;

Problem:
Sellers had data, but still couldn’t decide what to price. Tickets sat too high, recommendations didn’t feel trustworthy, and Smart Pricing wasn’t sticking.
Target audience:
Manual sellers on SeatGeek, desktop and mobile.
Goal:
Make the recommendation the thing sellers actually understand and act on.
Impact:
$980K incremental annual revenue. Selling rate +8.37%. Adoption +4.38%.

Overview

Pricing tickets to resell is hard.

Imagine you bought Knicks Game 5 tickets and couldn’t end up going. Imagine all the emotions and confusion you’d be facing while figuring out how to get rid of those tickets.

Selling tickets means guessing price, timing, and demand all at once. SeatGeek was recommending a price, but it wasn’t really working.

Problem

Users were not pricing competitively.

30% of listings above recommendation are less likely to sell. Confusion while pricing doesn’t just frustrate users.

It directly impacts selling and business success at SeatGeek.

  • Sellers priced too high.
  • They didn’t trust why we recommended a number.
  • Smart Pricing adoption stayed inconsistent.

Goals

What users needed vs what the business needed

Defining

First idea: give them more context

Iterating

Keep users in the recommended range

After interviews and user testing, users felt some parts of the new designs were confusing and unnecessary. So my new conviction was that we needed to keep users in our recommended range.

Visually grounding the user in our recommendation felt stronger than displaying data points and overwhelming the user.

Solution

Make the recommendation impossible to miss

Sellers needed one clear read:

Where the market is → Where they are → Where they should be

So I stopped adding insights and made the recommendation the product.

$980K

Estimated annualized revenue impact after shipping mobile and desktop. Sell-through +8.37%. Adoption +3.87% in a month.
