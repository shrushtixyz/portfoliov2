Redesigning Cursor’s onboarding for developers at every level

TLDR;

Problem:
Getting started in Cursor was confusing — unclear nav, weird terms, slow first project.
Target audience:
Developers at every level.
Goal:
Make first-run obvious, fast, and usable for beginners without blocking power users.
Impact:
Research + early redesign that clarified onboarding and got the team aligned on a first-run direction.

What I did

  • Ran research interviews and pulled out the real blockers.
  • Wireframed early onboarding directions.
  • Presented decks so the team could agree on a plan.

Overview

Does Cursor actually work for everyone?

Cursor says it’s for novice and experienced developers. We tested that claim: setup, first project, AI help, and whether the product felt understandable.

Problem

First-run was the failure mode

Using Cursor ourselves, the same issues kept repeating:

  • Onboarding was hard to follow
  • Features and terms were buried
  • Starting a new project took too long

Goals

  1. Clearer onboarding for new users
  2. Fewer hesitations mid-flow
  3. Faster path to a new project

Evaluative research

Competitive audit and early design exploration

Competitive audit

We looked at how other AI editors handle onboarding and first-run — what they show, what they hide, and how they get you coding.

How might we

How might we streamline Cursor’s onboarding and redesign the first-time experience to feel intuitive and efficient for developers?

Design version 1

First pass: clearer entry points, simpler nav, more guidance on day one. Expand the option space, then pick.

“Yeah, I definitely think adding these three buttons will benefit like beginners or people who are not very familiar with VS code who just want to start live coding.”

Insights

  • Search needs clearer cues when one action opens another layer.
  • Before signup, people want a plain explanation of what Cursor is.
  • Beginners need obvious start paths — not VS Code fluency on day one.

Solution

Guide the first run. Don’t dumb down the rest.

Make value clear before signup. Make “new project” obvious. Give beginners start buttons without hiding power-user flows.

Outcomes and reflections

What stuck

Onboarding has to work for different levels — and tiny copy/button changes can remove a lot of hesitation. Next: test with true beginners, bake accessibility in earlier, and keep measuring whether the first-run still holds as the product grows.
