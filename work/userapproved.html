Designing an AI product to create and manage synthetic users for ecommerce companies

TLDR;

Problem:
Onboarding was broken and the product felt cluttered — new teams couldn’t see value fast enough.
Target audience:
Ecommerce product teams using UserApproved.
Goal:
Fix first-run, clean up the system, and make the tool usable day one.
Impact:
Shipped 5+ features. First-time activation +40%.

What I did

  • Redesigned onboarding and activation.
  • Built a design system the whole product adopted.
  • Talked to 15+ early users and PMs, then turned that into Figma and code.
  • Shipped frontend changes in React/TypeScript with a team of 4.

Overview

An AI tool for simulating users

UserApproved helps teams pressure-test products with synthetic users. I joined as a design intern for three months — designing, prototyping, and shipping with engineers and early customers.

Problem

People couldn’t get in, and once they did, it was noisy

Three things were blocking the product:

  • Onboarding was confusing — new users didn’t know what to do first.
  • Dashboards and reports were overloaded.
  • No shared design system, so every screen felt different.

Exploring

Audit first, then talk to people

I mapped the product, marked friction, checked competitors, then used that as the interview script.

Defining

10 interviews. Same three themes.

I talked to QA, PMs, and designers. On a tiny team, hearing users directly was the scorecard — not our internal opinions.

  • Onboarding needs clearer steps and personalization.
  • Reports need a snapshot, not a wall of text.
  • The UI needs one system, not a pile of one-offs.

"I'd rather have that step by step. This is me. This is what I'm looking for, and then you give me what I want."

"If I click projects, is this the main page I see right away?"

"The first page to be, like, super complex. Like, over all the, you know, cognitive overload."

"I just want a quick snapshot of that. Of, like, percentages, maybe some graphs, make it look pretty. I don't want all the text."

"Having a good way to organize it, having a good way to maintain all of it without having to go to each and every single test case… that's also very important."

Design

Activation, then structure

Once we agreed on the plan, I mapped activation flows and brought options to the team before locking a direction.

Iterating

We cut scope to what users actually asked for

  1. Simpler onboarding and dashboard
  2. Clearer nav and project creation
  3. Reports that communicate impact fast

Solution

A system the product could grow on

I shipped the onboarding redesign, cleaned up IA and reports, and built a design system engineering could reuse. Activation went up 40%. More importantly, we had a shared structure — so the next features didn’t invent a new UI every time.
