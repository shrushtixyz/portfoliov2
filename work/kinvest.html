Designing a financial literacy app that helps kids learn money, investing, and banking

TLDR;

Problem:
Kids are curious about money. Most tools are built for adults, and school barely covers the hard parts.
Target audience:
Kids learning money skills, with parents in the loop.
Goal:
A family product where kids can practice budgeting and investing safely, and parents can see what’s happening.
Impact:
Defined strategy, IA, and onboarding for a kids finance app with simulated money practice.

What I did

  • Set the product direction for a kids finance platform.
  • Turned abstract money concepts into lessons kids can actually try.
  • Designed IA and onboarding.
  • Balanced kid autonomy with parent visibility.

Overview

Money skills, before bad habits stick

Kinvest teaches kids banking, cards, and stocks in a safe practice environment. I started it after watching my little brother try to make sense of money: curious, but stuck with adult tools and thin school coverage.

75% of U.S. students learn budgets in school. Only 22% learn compound interest, 19% credit card interest, 13% diversification ( OECD PISA 2022 ).

Problem

Parents want to teach money. The tools don’t help.

There’s almost nothing that teaches and lets kids practice, in language they understand.

Research

Start with the user, not the product

“Honestly, I’m not sure how to even explain building credit to my kid. No one ever taught me… I don’t know where to start, but I do believe in starting early.” Parent of a middle schooler

I defined a primary persona (Alex), mapped empathy, checked competitors, and wrote down the blockers that kept money learning out of reach.

Competitive Analysis

Pain points

  • Money concepts feel abstract and dense for his age.
  • School barely covers credit, investing, or taxes.
  • Parents often don’t feel equipped to teach it.
  • Adult finance apps are full of jargon and dead ends.

Analysis

Agree on the scorecard, then build

Problem

Alex needs a way to learn money that matches his age, before adulthood forces the lesson.

Goal

Teach budgeting, saving, investing, credit, and loans by letting kids:

  • Track goals
  • Simulate investing
  • Practice budgeting safely

Principles for the product

  • Age-right lessons: short, visual, try-it-now
  • Nav a kid can use without adult vocabulary
  • Parents can see progress without taking over
  • Check understanding with practice, not lectures

Information Architecture

Learn, bank, practice

Core areas: account, home, learn, pay, trade. Education next to real money practice, with parent oversight built in.

Wireframing

Lots of options, then a flow

I sketched onboarding, home, learn, pay, and trade, then mapped signup through the first lesson.

Solution

Practice money together

Kinvest lets kids learn stocks, cards, and loans by doing: track goals, simulate investing, practice budgeting, while parents stay in the loop.

Moving Forward

Next

  1. More advanced topics, still bite-sized
  2. Test with kids and parents on what actually teaches
  3. Keep iterating the UI around what makes learning stick
